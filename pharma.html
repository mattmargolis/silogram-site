Where publication gates access

Abstracts and data on file only go so far

Formulary, guideline, compendium and HTA reviews each prefer, or require, peer-reviewed evidence.

  • Formulary dossiers

    The AMCP Format prefers studies published in peer-reviewed journals. Congress abstracts, posters and data on file are what a dossier falls back on until the paper exists.1

  • P&T committees

    Medicare Part D guidance bases P&T decisions on scientific evidence, including peer-reviewed literature and clinical guidelines. Commercial plans decide a median of 4.2 months after approval.29

  • NCCN Category 1

    A Category 1 recommendation needs a peer-reviewed publication. Outside submissions are due 28 days before the panel’s annual meeting.34

  • Off-label use in Medicare

    Anticancer uses are medically accepted when listed in a recognized compendium or supported by peer-reviewed articles in the 26 journals CMS has named. At least one Medicare contractor asks for full articles, not abstracts.5

  • ICER reviews

    Reviews are timed to land near approval, and a sponsor’s unpublished data are redacted from the public report until they are published or presented, or 12 months pass.6

  • EU joint clinical assessment

    Since January 2025, new cancer medicines go through a joint assessment. The dossier is due within 100 days of the scope and includes the CSRs, protocols and SAPs, plus program code and output for analyses that a standard method can’t describe.1011

What Silogram does after a readout

Every document from the same locked analysis

One source for the whole burst

Top-line abstract, CSR to ICH E3, manuscript, CTD Module 2 summaries and payer dossiers draw on the same verified values. A number changes once and every document shows it.

A shorter internal loop

Methods drafted from the code, results linked to outputs, a number audit before every review round, and your house style learned from past papers. Reviewers spend their time on the argument, not the arithmetic.

Dates tied to access
