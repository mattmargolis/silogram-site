Abstracts and data on file only go so far.

Formulary, guideline, compendium and HTA reviews each prefer, or require, peer-reviewed evidence.

Formulary dossiers

The AMCP Format prefers studies published in peer-reviewed journals. Congress abstracts, posters and data on file are what a dossier falls back on until the paper exists.1

P&T committees

Medicare Part D guidance bases P&T decisions on scientific evidence, including peer-reviewed literature and clinical guidelines.2

NCCN Category 1

A Category 1 recommendation needs a peer-reviewed publication. Outside submissions are due 28 days before the panel's annual meeting.34

Off-label use in Medicare

Anticancer uses are medically accepted when listed in a recognized compendium or supported by peer-reviewed articles in the 26 journals CMS has named. At least one Medicare contractor asks for full articles, not abstracts.5

ICER reviews

Reviews are timed to land near approval, and a sponsor's unpublished data are redacted from the public report until they are published or presented, or 12 months pass.6

EU joint clinical assessment

Since January 2025, new cancer medicines go through a joint assessment. The dossier is due within 100 days of the scope and includes the CSRs, protocols and SAPs, plus program code and output for analyses a standard method can't describe.1011
